Tantu, meaning thread

A small thread-and-wire studio, growing one festival at a time.

Tantu Kala began as a Raksha Bandhan rakhi shop. It's since grown into a year-round handmade jewelry, décor and gifting studio — but the way we work hasn't changed: everything is still built by hand, from crochet thread or pipe cleaner, nothing else.

How it's made

One material rule, no exceptions

Every product in the Tantu Kala catalog — jewelry, home décor, car décor, office décor, gifting combos, rakhis, all of it — is made using only crochet thread or pipe cleaner. Not a mix of materials with a bit of crochet on top: those two, and nothing else. It's a deliberate constraint, not a limitation, and it's the reason two very different-looking pieces still feel like they came from the same hand.

Crochet thread

Worked stitch by stitch into jewelry, coasters, wall hoops, garlands and more — the same technique whether it's a pair of jhumkas or a mandap accent.

Pipe cleaner

Shaped and wrapped by hand for car charms, keychains, small figures and detailing — sturdier where a piece needs to hold its form.

Why we started

A rakhi order list that grew into a small studio

Tantu Kala started small, the way most handmade shops do — a handful of rakhi orders for family and friends around one Raksha Bandhan, made because storebought options felt generic. The response was enough to keep going the next year, and the year after that.

Somewhere along the way it stopped being a once-a-year thing. People started asking for jewelry, for something for their desk, for a gift box that didn't feel mass-produced. So we kept the same approach — crochet thread, pipe cleaner, made to order — and slowly widened what we made with it.

"We're still a small, handmade operation — not a factory, not a big team. That's less an apology and more the point."
